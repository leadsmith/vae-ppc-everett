Common Questions

Answers to What Families Ask Us Most

If you're still on the fence, these answers address the most common hesitations we hear from families just like yours.

How much does in-home elder care cost in Mukilteo, WA?

It depends on the level of care and how many hours your parent needs. Many families are surprised that starting with a few hours a week is far more affordable than moving a parent into a facility, and you only pay for the time you use. We give you a full breakdown during your free consultation, with no hidden fees. Some long-term care insurance policies and VA benefits also apply.

How quickly can care actually start?

In most cases, care can begin within 48–72 hours of your consultation. If it's urgent, like a parent or grandparent coming home from the hospital, tell us. We have a fast-start process for families who need help right away.

What if my parent refuses help or says they don't need it?

This is the most common concern we hear, and our care coordinators are experienced in this exact situation. We'll talk through approaches during your consultation, and we often suggest starting with just a few hours a week in a low-pressure way, like help around the house or a companion for errands. Many seniors who resisted at first now look forward to their caregiver's visits.

Can I trust a caregiver in my parent's home?

Every caregiver goes through criminal background checks, reference verification, and a skills assessment before they're placed. We carefully match each caregiver to your parent, and you can request a different one at any time, no explanation needed.

What does a caregiver actually help with?

Everyday non-medical help: meals, light housekeeping, laundry, personal care like bathing and dressing, medication reminders, errands, rides to appointments, and companionship. Care can be short-term, like after a hospital stay, or ongoing, and the plan covers exactly what your parent needs.

Do you provide dementia or Alzheimer's care?

Yes. We have caregivers specifically trained in dementia and memory care, including behavioral approaches, routine-building, safe redirection, and compassionate communication.

I don't live near my parent. Can you still help?

Yes. If you can't check in every day, a caregiver can be there on your parent's schedule, day or night, helping with meals, medication reminders, and keeping up the house. We stay in touch with your family throughout, so you're not left wondering how they're doing.

Why use an agency instead of hiring a private caregiver?

Hiring a caregiver privately often means your family handles everything: background checks, scheduling, payroll and taxes, and starting over if it doesn't work out. With us, every caregiver is screened, trained, and insured before they meet your parent, a local care coordinator checks in regularly, and if the match isn't right, we find someone else. Your parent still gets private, one-on-one care at home.
